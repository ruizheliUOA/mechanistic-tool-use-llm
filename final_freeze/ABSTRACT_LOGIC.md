# Abstract logic — frozen skeleton, not drafted

Five beats, in this order:

1. **Problem.** Tool-using models select the wrong action path *before* execution;
   these are structured, recurring directional errors.
2. **Method.** SAKIKO discovers model-specific error channels and selectively
   intervenes on their internal representations.
3. **Positive result.** A meaningful subset of these errors is behaviourally
   improvable, across multiple model families and scales, with structural
   specificity against random, reverse, wrong-layer and mismatched controls.
4. **The turn.** Aggregate improvement does not identify repair — a
   Net-positive intervention can break the majority of the correct samples it
   touches, and two interventions with near-identical aggregate gain can arrive at
   substantially different destinations.
5. **Resolution.** Destination-resolved verification and evidence-graded licensing
   assign only the strongest claim the evidence supports — and **decline in
   practice**: two of three prospectively evaluated models were formally declined
   despite favourable point estimates, because their intervals were too wide.

## Hard constraints

- **Do not lead with activation steering novelty.** Beat 2 is method description,
  not a novelty claim.
- Do not put the licence count in the abstract. If the ADMIT/DECLINE split is
  mentioned, the DECLINEs must appear with it — never the ADMIT alone.
- Never imply the declined models are uncorrectable.
- Do not use "reliably fixes", "controlled scaling study", or any intrinsic
  model-correctability phrasing.
- Beat 4 must carry **both** examples — the preservation failure alone reads as a
  single-model anomaly; with the score-space comparison it reads as a
  methodological finding.
- **Write the abstract last**, from the finished Section 5.
