## The surviving object

> Channel-specific, multiclass tool-decision intervention adjudication that separates internal
> readability, causal specificity, target destination, preservation, and evidential licensing.

Coherent as a contribution rather than a checklist because the components are **not
independently substitutable**: the destination accounting is only definable given automatic
channel discovery over `A_D` with `|A_D| ≥ 3`; the licensing layer is only meaningful given a
causal null rather than a calibration set; and the object adjudicated — a claim about a causal
manipulation — is different in kind from the prediction risk that selective prediction and
conformal risk control bound.

## The strongest fully accurate novelty sentence

The candidate sentence implies preservation was a binding formal condition for the near-positive
cases. **It was not** — Qwen3-4B and Gemma were refused on destination intervals; preservation
entered retrospectively. The accurate form:

> Direction-specific and aggregate-positive intervention effects do not, by themselves, establish
> a licensed correction claim: under the frozen protocol, near-positive settings were still
> refused when destination evidence remained insufficient, while a retrospective
> exposure-conditional analysis exposed preservation risk not visible in aggregate summaries.

Every clause verified: direction-specific (0/59 randoms, reverse null), aggregate-positive
(Net +39, +16), formal refusal (conditions 3 and 6), multiclass destination (27 of 64 and 10 of
27 to a third wrong action), preservation (retrospective, labelled as such), prospective
adjudication (sealed one-shot).

## Destination value

```
DESTINATION_VALUE: CONCEPTUALLY_LOAD_BEARING_BUT_EMPIRICALLY_UNTRIGGERED
```

It did not change an observed formal decision beyond specificity. It **is** triggered in
simulation — "many exits, wrong destination" (80 exits, 25 gold, 55 other-wrong) passes
specificity and preservation and is caught by the destination conditions alone — and it changes
*interpretation* in every reported setting even where it does not change the verdict. The
manuscript states this in both Results and Discussion; it must not be upgraded.
