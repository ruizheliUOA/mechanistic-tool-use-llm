## What may be claimed

Nothing new. The panel produced **no scientific evidence**, so no allowed claim from §20
(`correctability varies across host-model settings`; `the licensing protocol produces different
admissibility outcomes across models`; `a cross-family result extends model breadth`) gains any
new support from it.

## What must not be claimed — including about this panel itself

The forbidden list is unchanged: no scaling law, no "architecture X causes correctability", no
"larger models are more correctable", no "Qwen is more correctable than Gemma", no universal
cross-model generalisation. With the settings actually available, architecture is not causally
isolated.

**One addition specific to this outcome.** A blocked slot is **not** a negative result. It must
never be written or implied that Gemma or Qwen3.5 "failed", "did not replicate", "showed no
eligible channel", or "could not support a licence". Nothing was measured. The only defensible
statement is that the panel was attempted and could not be executed for mechanical reasons
external to the science.

## The one thing this attempt does establish

It is procedural, not scientific, and it is worth a sentence in the manuscript's limitations
only if the authors want it: the panel was **frozen before inference**, both slots were closed
against replacement, and when both slots turned out to be mechanically unreachable the
protocol's response was to return nothing rather than to substitute an obtainable model. That
is the firewall working as designed. It is not evidence about correctability.
